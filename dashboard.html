12-month forecast, by series

52 weeks past the end of the dataset (2012-10-26 → 2013-10-25), using each series' own selection-corrected model. Shaded band is forecast ± z·σₑ — the same forecast-error std that sizes this series' safety stock, so the band shown here is literally what that safety stock is meant to absorb.

Assumption, stated plainly: this is real forecasting into the unknown, not a backtest — actuals for these dates don't exist yet. LightGBM needs future values of holiday timing, markdown activity, temperature, fuel price, CPI, and unemployment that haven't happened. Every one of those is carried forward from the same calendar week one year earlier (the same logic Seasonal Naive already uses for demand itself). A real deployment would use an actual promotional calendar and macro forecast instead of this proxy.

Methodology

Full write-up (data, model selection rationale, formulas, limitations) is in the accompanying documentation. Summary:

Models compared
Naive (lag-1) · Seasonal naive (lag-52) · Holt-Winters exponential smoothing (local, per series) · LightGBM (global, pooled across all 15 series with real exogenous covariates)
Evaluation
12-week holdout per series, WMAE weighted 5× on holiday weeks — the same metric the real Kaggle competition used
Selection fix
Picking a single "best" model by WMAE (Holt-Winters) and applying it to every series agrees with picking the lowest-std model per series on only 7 of 15 series (47%). Accuracy and consistency are different properties — a model can win on one and lose on the other — and since safety stock depends on std, not WMAE, the plan now selects each series' model by std directly rather than defaulting to the network-wide accuracy winner.
Safety stock
SS = z · σₑ · √L, where σₑ is that series' selected-model backtest forecast-error std
Reorder point
ROP = (avg weekly demand × L) + SS
Assumptions
95% target service level (z = 1.645) · 2-week replenishment lead time (not in the dataset; stated assumption)
Data
Real: Walmart Recruiting – Store Sales Forecasting (Kaggle, 2014). 421,570 weekly Store×Dept records, Feb 2010–Oct 2012, with real temperature, fuel price, CPI, unemployment, and promotional markdown data. Scoped to the 5 highest-volume stores × 3 highest-volume, fully-populated departments (15 series, no missing weeks).
12-month forecast
Each series' selected model refit on its full history (no holdout) and projected 52 weeks past the dataset's end. Naive and Seasonal Naive need no new assumptions (the latter's "last year" lookback is real observed data even 52 weeks out). Holt-Winters extrapolates its own trend/seasonal decomposition. LightGBM alone needs future covariates that don't exist yet — carried forward from the same calendar week one year prior, stated as an explicit assumption rather than hidden.
